# Record the workshop narration

Use the supplied twelve-section script with delivery cues. Read only the spoken
sentences, not headings, bracketed instructions or section numbers. The recording
is a replacement narration, not a change to scientific findings.

## Setup

- A phone voice recorder or a computer microphone is enough. Use the quietest
  room available; curtains and soft furnishings help reduce echo.
- Keep the microphone about 15–20 cm from your mouth, slightly to one side,
  and keep that distance steady. Silence notifications and avoid touching the mic.
- Record a short test and listen with headphones before doing the whole script.
  If speech distorts, lower the recording level or move back slightly.
- WAV is preferred when available; M4A is acceptable. Send the original audio,
  without background music or added noise reduction.

## Delivery

- Speak naturally to one colleague. Keep your normal accent; no trailer voice.
- Leave five seconds of quiet room sound at the beginning and end.
- Pause two seconds after each paragraph and three seconds between sections.
- If you stumble, pause three seconds and repeat the complete sentence.
  Leave both takes in the file; editing can remove the unwanted one.
- Bold words in the script suggest light emphasis, not increased volume.
- One continuous file or twelve numbered section files both work. The visuals
  will follow the recording; do not rush to match the old video's duration.

After recording, send the original file(s) privately for editing. The plan is
gentle cleanup, take selection, level balancing, animation retiming and new
toggleable subtitles, followed by author review. No recording has been received
yet; none of those production steps is complete. Voice references and raw takes
are not repository assets, and YouTube publication requires separate approval.
